Inside one of your muscle cells

  • Working mitochondria
  • Worn mitochondria
  • Urolithin A
  • Recycling membrane

From your thirties, your mitochondria start to wear out.

They're the engines in every muscle cell. Worn ones make less energy and leak more waste, and the cell gets slower at clearing them. You feel it as heavier legs and longer recoveries.

Urolithin A flags them for recycling.

It switches mitophagy back on, the cell's own clean-up: each worn mitochondrion is sealed in a membrane, broken down, and its parts reused.

New ones take their place.

Fresh, efficient mitochondria grow in the space the worn ones left. This is the renewal the trials measured.
